> "To fear love is to fear life, and those who fear life are already three parts dead."—Bertrand Russell

Matt Haig's The Midnight Library is a fast, fable-like meditation on that fear. Nora Seed, crushed by regret, finds herself in a library between life and death where each book opens onto a different version of her life—each "if only" made real. As she tries on alternate futures that look perfect, impressive, or finally "fixed," the story keeps returning to a few questions:

- What do we really mean by a "different" life—more success, more love, more certainty, less pain?
- Why can a life that shines from the outside feel empty from within?
- How do shame and comparison narrow our attention until we miss small joys and ordinary kindness?
- What does it mean to make peace with a life—not as resignation, but as presence?
